## 1. Introduction and Learning Objectives {#introduction}

### Overview

Obstetric and gynecologic radiology represents a highly specialized field requiring expertise in multiple imaging modalities, comprehensive understanding of reproductive anatomy and physiology, and awareness of radiation safety considerations unique to women of reproductive age. This educational resource is designed to provide radiology residents with expert-level knowledge in OB/GYN imaging.

### Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this educational resource, residents will be able to:

1. **Technical Proficiency**: Demonstrate mastery of imaging techniques specific to obstetric and gynecologic conditions
2. **Anatomical Expertise**: Identify normal and variant anatomy of the female reproductive system across all age groups
3. **Pathological Recognition**: Diagnose and differentiate common and uncommon pathological conditions
4. **Protocol Optimization**: Select appropriate imaging protocols for specific clinical scenarios
5. **Safety Management**: Apply radiation safety principles in pregnant and potentially pregnant patients
6. **Emergency Assessment**: Rapidly evaluate emergency conditions in obstetric and gynecologic patients
7. **Multidisciplinary Communication**: Effectively communicate findings to obstetricians, gynecologists, and other healthcare providers

## 2. Imaging Physics and Technical Considerations {#imaging-physics}

### 2.1 Ultrasound Physics in OB/GYN

#### Basic Principles

Ultrasound imaging in obstetrics and gynecology relies on the piezoelectric effect, where mechanical pressure creates electrical charges in crystalline materials. The fundamental equation governing ultrasound propagation is:

**v = fλ**

Where:
- v = velocity of sound in tissue (approximately 1540 m/s in soft tissue)
- f = frequency
- λ = wavelength

#### Frequency Selection

**Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS)**:
- Frequency: 5-9 MHz
- Higher frequency provides superior near-field resolution
- Limited penetration depth (suitable for pelvic organs)
- Optimal for early pregnancy assessment and detailed pelvic evaluation

**Transabdominal Ultrasound (TAUS)**:
- Frequency: 2-5 MHz
- Lower frequency provides greater penetration
- Essential for late pregnancy assessment and large mass evaluation
- Requires full bladder for optimal pelvic visualization

#### Doppler Physics

**Doppler Effect Equation**:
```
fd = 2f₀(v cos θ)/c
```

Where:
- fd = Doppler frequency shift
- f₀ = transmitted frequency
- v = velocity of moving reflector
- θ = angle between ultrasound beam and direction of motion
- c = speed of sound in tissue

**Clinical Applications**:
- **Power Doppler**: Angle-independent, sensitive to low-flow states
- **Color Doppler**: Angle-dependent, directional flow information
- **Spectral Doppler**: Quantitative flow assessment with waveform analysis

#### Advanced Ultrasound Techniques

**Harmonic Imaging**:
- Utilizes tissue harmonic frequencies (2f₀, 3f₀)
- Improved contrast resolution
- Reduced artifact burden
- Enhanced visualization of fetal structures

**3D/4D Ultrasound**:
- Volume acquisition and reconstruction
- Surface rendering for fetal facial features
- Multiplanar reconstruction for anatomical assessment
- 4D provides real-time 3D imaging

**Elastography**:
- Tissue stiffness assessment
- Strain elastography vs. shear wave elastography
- Applications in cervical length assessment and liver fibrosis evaluation

### 2.2 MRI Physics in OB/GYN

#### Basic MRI Principles

**Magnetic Resonance Equation**:
```
f = γB₀/2π
```

Where:
- f = resonant frequency (Larmor frequency)
- γ = gyromagnetic ratio (42.58 MHz/T for hydrogen)
- B₀ = magnetic field strength

#### Tissue Contrast in Pelvic Imaging

**T1 Relaxation**:
- Spin-lattice relaxation
- Determines T1-weighted image contrast
- Fat appears bright on T1WI
- Useful for anatomical delineation and hemorrhage detection

**T2 Relaxation**:
- Spin-spin relaxation
- Fluid appears bright on T2WI
- Excellent soft tissue contrast
- Primary sequence for pelvic pathology assessment

#### Specialized MRI Sequences

**Fast Spin Echo (FSE) T2-Weighted Imaging**:
- Rapid acquisition with maintained T2 contrast
- Essential for pelvic anatomy visualization
- Fat suppression improves pathology conspicuity

**Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI)**:
- Sensitive to tissue microstructure
- Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC) quantification
- Valuable for characterizing pelvic masses and detecting malignancy

**Dynamic Contrast Enhancement (DCE)**:
- Gadolinium-based contrast agent administration
- Time-intensity curve analysis
- Perfusion assessment and lesion characterization

**MR Angiography**:
- Time-of-flight (TOF) technique
- Contrast-enhanced MRA for vascular assessment
- Important for evaluating pelvic vascular anatomy and pathology

#### Safety Considerations in MRI

**Gadolinium in Pregnancy**:
- FDA Pregnancy Category C
- Crosses placental barrier
- Should be avoided unless potential benefits outweigh risks
- Alternative: high-resolution T2-weighted sequences

**Acoustic Noise**:
- Sound levels can exceed 100 dB
- Potential hearing protection requirements
- Gradient echo sequences typically louder than spin echo

**Specific Absorption Rate (SAR)**:
- RF energy deposition in tissue
- FDA limit: 4 W/kg for whole body
- Particular concern in pregnancy due to developing fetal tissues

### 2.3 CT Physics and Radiation Dosimetry

#### CT Image Formation

**Attenuation Coefficient**:
```
I = I₀e^(-μx)
```

Where:
- I = transmitted intensity
- I₀ = incident intensity
- μ = linear attenuation coefficient
- x = thickness of material

#### Radiation Dose Metrics

**Computed Tomography Dose Index (CTDI)**:
- Standardized dose measurement
- CTDI₁₀₀: dose from single axial scan over 100 mm
- CTDIvol: volume-averaged dose index
- Dose Length Product (DLP) = CTDIvol × scan length

**Effective Dose**:
- Whole-body dose equivalent
- Accounts for tissue radiosensitivity
- Expressed in millisieverts (mSv)
- Critical for risk assessment in pregnancy

#### Dose Reduction Techniques

**Automatic Exposure Control (AEC)**:
- Real-time tube current modulation
- Maintains image quality while minimizing dose
- Particularly important in pelvic imaging

**Iterative Reconstruction**:
- Advanced image reconstruction algorithms
- Significant dose reduction potential (30-60%)
- Maintains diagnostic image quality

**Organ-Specific Shielding**:
- Bismuth breast shields
- Gonadal shielding when not interfering with diagnostic area
- Careful positioning to avoid artifact creation

## 3. Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology {#ultrasound}

### 3.1 Technical Optimization

#### Equipment Setup and Patient Preparation

**Transabdominal Approach**:
- Patient preparation: Full bladder (300-500 mL)
- Probe selection: 2-5 MHz curved array
- Gel application: Liberal amount for optimal acoustic coupling
- Patient positioning: Supine with head slightly elevated

**Transvaginal Approach**:
- Patient preparation: Empty bladder
- Probe selection: 5-9 MHz endocavitary probe
- Probe preparation: Sterile cover and coupling gel
- Patient positioning: Lithotomy or modified lithotomy

#### Image Optimization Parameters

**Gain Settings**:
- Overall gain: Adjust for optimal tissue brightness
- Time gain compensation (TGC): Uniform brightness throughout depth
- Dynamic range: Typically 60-80 dB for general imaging

**Frequency and Focus**:
- Penetration vs. resolution trade-off
- Multiple focal zones for uniform beam quality
- Compound imaging for speckle reduction

**Measurement Accuracy**:
- Proper gain settings for edge detection
- Perpendicular angle to structure of interest
- Freeze frame before measurement
- Multiple measurements for accuracy

### 3.2 Standard Examination Protocols

#### First Trimester Ultrasound Protocol

**Transvaginal Approach (Preferred)**:

1. **Uterine Survey**:
   - Sagittal and transverse planes
   - Endometrial thickness measurement
   - Myometrial assessment for fibroids or anomalies

2. **Gestational Sac Assessment**:
   - Mean sac diameter (MSD): (L + W + H)/3
   - Gestational sac volume
   - Decidual reaction thickness
   - Double decidual sac sign

3. **Embryonic/Fetal Biometry**:
   - Crown-rump length (CRL): Most accurate dating parameter
   - Biparietal diameter (BPD) after 10 weeks
   - Nuchal translucency (NT) at 11-14 weeks

4. **Cardiac Assessment**:
   - M-mode for heart rate documentation
   - Color Doppler for cardiac activity
   - Normal range: 120-180 bpm after 6 weeks

5. **Adnexal Evaluation**:
   - Ovarian morphology and volume
   - Corpus luteum identification
   - Assessment for adnexal masses

#### Second and Third Trimester Protocol

**Comprehensive Fetal Survey**:

1. **Biometric Measurements**:
   - Biparietal diameter (BPD)
   - Head circumference (HC)
   - Abdominal circumference (AC)
   - Femur length (FL)
   - Estimated fetal weight (EFW)

2. **Anatomical Survey**:
   - **Cranial**: Lateral ventricles, choroid plexus, cerebellum
   - **Cardiac**: Four-chamber view, outflow tracts
   - **Thoracic**: Lung echogenicity, diaphragm continuity
   - **Abdominal**: Stomach, kidneys, bladder, cord insertion
   - **Skeletal**: Long bones, spine, hands, feet

3. **Placental Assessment**:
   - Location and relationship to cervical os
   - Thickness and echogenicity
   - Cord insertion site
   - Number of cord vessels

4. **Amniotic Fluid Volume**:
   - Amniotic fluid index (AFI)
   - Maximum vertical pocket (MVP)
   - Oligohydramnios: AFI <5 cm or MVP <2 cm
   - Polyhydramnios: AFI >25 cm or MVP >8 cm

### 3.3 Doppler Studies in OB/GYN

#### Obstetric Doppler Applications

**Uterine Artery Doppler**:
- Performed at 20-24 weeks gestation
- Waveform analysis: Pulsatility index (PI), resistance index (RI)
- Predictive value for preeclampsia and IUGR
- Normal: Progressive decrease in resistance with advancing gestation

**Umbilical Artery Doppler**:
- Essential for fetal surveillance in high-risk pregnancies
- Absent or reversed end-diastolic flow: Severe placental insufficiency
- Cerebroplacental ratio: Middle cerebral artery PI / Umbilical artery PI

**Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) Doppler**:
- Assessment of fetal anemia (peak systolic velocity)
- Brain sparing effect in IUGR
- Reference ranges vary with gestational age

#### Gynecologic Doppler Applications

**Ovarian Mass Characterization**:
- Color Doppler for vascularity assessment
- Spectral Doppler for impedance indices
- Malignancy predictors: Low RI (<0.4), high diastolic flow

**Endometrial Assessment**:
- Spiral artery flow in menstrual cycle evaluation
- Subendometrial blood flow in fertility assessment
- Power Doppler sensitivity for low-flow states

### 3.4 3D/4D Ultrasound Technology

#### Technical Principles

**Volume Acquisition**:
- Automated probe movement through predetermined angle
- Free-hand acquisition with position sensing
- Real-time 4D processing

**Rendering Modes**:
- **Surface rendering**: Optimal for fetal facial features
- **Maximum intensity projection**: Vascular structures
- **Minimum intensity projection**: Fluid-filled structures
- **Multiplanar reconstruction**: Simultaneous orthogonal views

#### Clinical Applications

**Fetal Applications**:
- Facial anomaly detection (cleft lip/palate)
- Neural tube defect assessment
- Limb deformity evaluation
- Parent bonding enhancement

**Gynecologic Applications**:
- Uterine anomaly classification
- Intrauterine device localization
- Endometrial volume measurement
- Tubal patency assessment (HyCoSy)

## 4. Magnetic Resonance Imaging in OB/GYN {#mri}

### 4.1 MRI Protocols and Sequences

#### Standard Pelvic MRI Protocol

**Patient Preparation**:
- Fasting: 4-6 hours to reduce bowel motion
- Bladder: Partially full for anatomical reference
- Antispasmodic agents: Glucagon or hyoscine butylbromide
- Patient positioning: Supine, feet first

**Sequence Selection**:

1. **T2-Weighted FSE**:
   - **Sagittal plane**: Uterine anatomy overview
   - **Axial oblique**: Perpendicular to uterine axis
   - **Coronal oblique**: Parallel to uterine axis
   - Parameters: TR 4000-6000 ms, TE 90-120 ms

2. **T1-Weighted Sequences**:
   - **Pre-contrast**: Hemorrhage and fat identification
   - **Post-contrast**: Dynamic enhancement patterns
   - Fat saturation for enhanced lesion conspicuity

3. **Diffusion-Weighted Imaging**:
   - Multiple b-values: 0, 400, 800, 1000 s/mm²
   - ADC map generation for quantitative analysis
   - Valuable for lesion characterization and treatment response

#### Advanced MRI Techniques

**MR Spectroscopy**:
- Single-voxel or chemical shift imaging
- Metabolite assessment: Choline, creatine, lactate
- Emerging role in ovarian cancer characterization

**Perfusion Imaging**:
- Dynamic susceptibility contrast (DSC)
- Dynamic contrast enhancement (DCE)
- Arterial spin labeling (ASL) - no contrast required

**High-Resolution T2 Mapping**:
- Quantitative T2 relaxometry
- Tissue characterization applications
- Research applications in fibroid treatment

### 4.2 Normal Pelvic Anatomy on MRI

#### Uterine Anatomy

**Zonal Anatomy**:
- **Endometrium**: High T2 signal, varies with menstrual cycle
- **Junctional Zone**: Low T2 signal, inner myometrium
- **Outer Myometrium**: Intermediate T2 signal
- **Serosa**: Thin high T2 signal layer

**Menstrual Cycle Variations**:
- **Menstrual phase**: Thin endometrium, debris in cavity
- **Proliferative phase**: Thin endometrium, prominent junctional zone
- **Secretory phase**: Thick endometrium, high T2 signal
- **Postmenopausal**: Atrophic endometrium, thin junctional zone

#### Ovarian Anatomy

**Signal Characteristics**:
- **Cortex**: Hypointense T2 signal
- **Medulla**: Hyperintense T2 signal
- **Follicles**: High T2 signal, vary with cycle phase
- **Corpus luteum**: Complex signal, hemorrhagic components

**Age-Related Changes**:
- **Reproductive age**: 3-5 cm in diameter
- **Postmenopausal**: <2 cm diameter, decreased follicles
- **Pediatric**: Small, predominantly cortical tissue

### 4.3 Contrast Agents and Safety

#### Gadolinium-Based Contrast Agents (GBCAs)

**Classification**:
- **Linear agents**: Gadopentetate dimeglumine (Magnevist)
- **Macrocyclic agents**: Gadobutrol (Gadavist), Gadoterate meglumine (Dotarem)
- **Hepatocyte-specific**: Gadoxetic acid (Eovist/Primovist)

**Pharmacokinetics**:
- Extracellular distribution
- Renal elimination (t½ = 1.5 hours)
- No metabolism
- Crosses blood-brain barrier minimally

**Safety Considerations**:
- **Nephrogenic systemic fibrosis**: Risk with linear agents in severe CKD
- **Gadolinium retention**: Preference for macrocyclic agents
- **Pregnancy**: Avoid unless absolutely necessary
- **Breastfeeding**: Brief interruption recommended (24-48 hours)

#### Alternative Contrast Strategies

**High-Resolution T2-Weighted Imaging**:
- Excellent soft tissue contrast without gadolinium
- Sufficient for most gynecologic pathology
- Preferred approach in pregnancy and renal impairment

**Diffusion-Weighted Imaging**:
- Functional imaging without contrast administration
- Valuable for lesion characterization
- Monitoring treatment response

## 5. Computed Tomography in OB/GYN {#ct}

### 5.1 CT Protocol Optimization

#### Pelvic CT Protocols

**Routine Pelvic CT**:
- **Coverage**: Diaphragm to symphysis pubis
- **Slice thickness**: 2.5-5 mm reconstruction
- **Contrast timing**: 70-80 seconds after injection
- **Contrast volume**: 100-150 mL at 2-3 mL/s

**Triple-Phase CT**:
- **Unenhanced phase**: Baseline attenuation values
- **Arterial phase**: 25-30 seconds post-injection
- **Portal venous phase**: 70-80 seconds post-injection
- **Delayed phase**: 5-10 minutes (when indicated)

#### Dose Optimization Strategies

**Technical Parameters**:
- **kVp selection**: 100-120 kVp for pelvic imaging
- **mAs modulation**: Automatic exposure control
- **Pitch**: 0.8-1.2 for optimal image quality
- **Reconstruction algorithms**: Iterative reconstruction for dose reduction

**Patient-Specific Considerations**:
- **Body habitus**: Larger patients require higher technique
- **Age**: Pediatric protocols with significant dose reduction
- **Pregnancy**: Strict indication requirements and shielding

### 5.2 CT Contrast Considerations

#### Iodinated Contrast Agents

**Types**:
- **Ionic**: High osmolality, increased adverse reaction rate
- **Non-ionic**: Lower osmolality, preferred for most applications
- **Iso-osmolar**: Iodixanol, lowest osmolality

**Administration Protocols**:
- **IV access**: 18-20 gauge for adequate flow rates
- **Pre-medication**: For patients with prior contrast reactions
- **Hydration**: Pre and post-contrast for nephroprotection

**Safety Profiles**:
- **Immediate reactions**: 0.6% with non-ionic agents
- **Delayed reactions**: Skin reactions 1-7 days post-administration
- **Contrast-induced nephropathy**: Risk factors include CKD, diabetes, dehydration

#### Oral Contrast Agents

**Positive contrast**:
- **Barium sulfate**: High atomic number, excellent bowel opacification
- **Iodinated agents**: Water-soluble, absorbed if perforation occurs

**Neutral contrast**:
- **Water**: Negative contrast for stomach
- **Low-density barium**: Improved mucosal visualization

### 5.3 Emergency CT Applications

#### Acute Pelvic Pain

**Clinical Scenarios**:
- Ovarian torsion
- Appendicitis in pregnancy
- Ectopic pregnancy complications
- Pelvic inflammatory disease

**CT Advantages**:
- Rapid acquisition
- Excellent visualization of complications
- Assessment of alternative diagnoses
- Emergency department availability

#### CT in Pregnancy

**Indications**:
- Maternal trauma
- Acute appendicitis
- Pulmonary embolism
- Urolithiasis

**Radiation Considerations**:
- Fetal dose calculation required
- Counseling regarding risks and benefits
- Alternative imaging modalities consideration
- Documentation of medical necessity

## 6. Nuclear Medicine and PET Imaging {#nuclear-medicine}

### 6.1 Nuclear Medicine in Gynecology

#### Radiopharmaceuticals

**Technetium-99m Agents**:
- **Tc-99m MIBI**: Breast imaging, parathyroid adenoma
- **Tc-99m MAA**: Pulmonary embolism assessment
- **Tc-99m MDP**: Bone metastases screening

**Gallium-67**:
- Infection and inflammation imaging
- Pelvic inflammatory disease assessment
- Limited use due to poor imaging characteristics

#### Sentinel Lymph Node Mapping

**Technique**:
- **Tc-99m sulfur colloid**: Lymphatic mapping
- **Patent blue dye**: Visual identification
- **Combined approach**: Improved detection sensitivity

**Applications**:
- **Cervical cancer**: Stage IA2-IB1 disease
- **Endometrial cancer**: High-grade histology
- **Vulvar cancer**: Early-stage disease

### 6.2 PET Imaging in Gynecologic Oncology

#### FDG-PET Physics

**Fluorine-18 Fluorodeoxyglucose**:
- Glucose analog with 18F substitution
- Half-life: 110 minutes
- Accumulates in metabolically active tissues
- Trapped after hexokinase phosphorylation

**Image Acquisition**:
- **Patient preparation**: 6-hour fast, glucose <200 mg/dL
- **Injection to imaging interval**: 60-90 minutes
- **Acquisition time**: 2-4 minutes per bed position
- **Attenuation correction**: CT-based preferred

#### Clinical Applications

**Staging**:
- **Cervical cancer**: Nodal assessment, distant metastases
- **Endometrial cancer**: High-grade tumors, recurrence
- **Ovarian cancer**: Peritoneal carcinomatosis, recurrence

**Treatment Response**:
- **Early metabolic changes**: Before anatomical changes
- **Quantitative analysis**: SUVmax, metabolic tumor volume
- **Residual disease assessment**: Post-chemotherapy evaluation

#### Interpretation Pitfalls

**Physiologic Uptake**:
- **Brown fat**: Symmetric neck and shoulder uptake
- **Muscle uptake**: Related to recent activity or anxiety
- **Urinary activity**: Bladder and collecting system
- **Ovarian uptake**: Corpus luteum, ovulation-related

**False Positives**:
- **Inflammation**: Post-surgical changes, infection
- **Granulomatous disease**: Sarcoidosis, tuberculosis
- **Benign tumors**: Some adenomas, leiomyomas

## 7. Interventional Radiology in Gynecology {#interventional}

### 7.1 Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)

#### Patient Selection

**Indications**:
- Symptomatic uterine fibroids
- Adenomyosis-related bleeding
- Post-partum hemorrhage
- Pelvic arteriovenous malformations

**Contraindications**:
- **Absolute**: Pregnancy, active pelvic infection, known gynecologic malignancy
- **Relative**: Desire for future pregnancy, small submucosal fibroids
- **Technical**: Severe atherosclerotic disease, contrast allergy

#### Technical Procedure

**Pre-procedural Imaging**:
- **MRI**: Fibroid mapping, adenomyosis assessment
- **Doppler ultrasound**: Baseline uterine artery flow
- **Laboratory studies**: CBC, coagulation studies, pregnancy test

**Procedural Technique**:
1. **Arterial access**: Common femoral artery approach
2. **Catheter selection**: 4-5 Fr diagnostic, 5-6 Fr microcatheter
3. **Selective catheterization**: Both uterine arteries
4. **Embolization**: Polyvinyl alcohol particles, tris-acryl gelatin microspheres
5. **End point**: Near-stasis of forward flow

**Embolic Agents**:
- **PVA particles**: 350-500 μm for fibroids
- **Microspheres**: More uniform size distribution
- **Coils**: Large vessel occlusion, AVM treatment

#### Post-procedural Management

**Immediate Post-procedure**:
- **Pain management**: Patient-controlled analgesia
- **Antiemetics**: Ondansetron, promethazine
- **Monitoring**: Vital signs, access site assessment
- **Discharge criteria**: Stable vital signs, adequate pain control

**Follow-up Protocol**:
- **Clinical assessment**: Symptom improvement at 3-6 months
- **Imaging**: MRI at 3-6 months for fibroid shrinkage
- **Complications**: Infection, amenorrhea, ovarian dysfunction

### 7.2 Ovarian Vein Embolization

#### Pelvic Congestion Syndrome

**Clinical Presentation**:
- Chronic pelvic pain exacerbated by prolonged standing
- Post-coital aching
- Dyspareunia and dysmenorrhea
- Visible pelvic varicosities

**Diagnostic Imaging**:
- **CT venography**: Dilated ovarian veins (>8 mm diameter)
- **MR venography**: Non-invasive alternative
- **Conventional venography**: Gold standard, therapeutic option

#### Embolization Technique

**Access and Catheterization**:
- **Right jugular approach**: Direct access to right ovarian vein
- **Femoral approach**: May require renal vein catheterization for left side
- **Catheter selection**: 5 Fr straight or angled catheters

**Embolization Strategy**:
- **Coil embolization**: Primary embolic agent
- **Sclerotherapy**: Sodium tetradecyl sulfate foam
- **Combined approach**: Coils plus sclerosant

### 7.3 Fallopian Tube Recanalization

#### Proximal Tubal Occlusion

**Etiology**:
- Inflammatory debris
- Mucus plugs
- Spasm
- True anatomical obstruction

**Patient Selection**:
- Infertility with documented proximal tubal occlusion
- Normal distal tubal patency
- Absence of pelvic inflammatory disease

#### Technique

**Hysterosalpingography**:
- **Initial assessment**: Tubal patency evaluation
- **Selective catheterization**: 3 Fr coaxial microcatheter
- **Guidewire manipulation**: 0.018" hydrophilic wire
- **Success criteria**: Free spill of contrast into peritoneal cavity

**Alternative Approaches**:
- **Balloon tuboplasty**: Controlled dilation
- **Falloposcopy**: Direct visualization and treatment

## 8. Radiation Safety and Pregnancy {#radiation-safety}

### 8.1 Radiation Biology and Fetal Development

#### Radiation Effects on Development

**Critical Periods**:
- **Pre-implantation** (0-2 weeks): All-or-nothing effect
- **Organogenesis** (2-8 weeks): Malformation risk
- **Fetal period** (8+ weeks): Growth restriction, CNS effects

**Dose-Response Relationships**:
- **Threshold dose for malformations**: 100-200 mGy
- **Growth restriction**: >250 mGy
- **Intellectual disability**: >300 mGy during 8-15 weeks
- **Cancer risk**: Linear, no threshold model

#### Specific Organ Sensitivities

**Central Nervous System**:
- Most radiosensitive during 8-15 weeks
- Microcephaly risk with doses >300 mGy
- IQ reduction: 25 points per Gy exposure

**Eyes**:
- Cataract formation with doses >2000 mGy
- Sensitive period: 4-7 weeks gestation

**Thyroid**:
- Iodine-131 readily crosses placenta
- Fetal thyroid functional after 12 weeks
- Alternative isotopes preferred

### 8.2 Dose Calculations and Risk Assessment

#### Common Examination Doses

**Diagnostic Radiology**:
- **Chest X-ray**: <0.1 mGy fetal dose
- **Abdominal X-ray**: 1-4 mGy fetal dose
- **Pelvic CT**: 10-50 mGy fetal dose
- **CT pulmonary angiogram**: 0.2-0.7 mGy fetal dose

**Nuclear Medicine**:
- **Tc-99m bone scan**: 4-5 mGy fetal dose
- **I-131 thyroid scan**: 58 mGy fetal dose
- **FDG-PET**: 10-20 mGy fetal dose

#### Risk Communication

**Background Cancer Risk**:
- Childhood cancer incidence: 1 in 600-700
- Radiation-induced increase: 0.06% per 10 mGy
- Most examinations result in minimal risk increase

**Counseling Points**:
- Actual vs. perceived risk discussion
- Medical necessity of examination
- Alternative imaging modalities
- Delayed examination feasibility

### 8.3 Optimization Strategies

#### Technical Modifications

**CT Protocol Adjustments**:
- **Reduced mAs**: Accept increased noise for non-critical diagnoses
- **Increased pitch**: Faster scanning, reduced dose
- **Limited coverage**: Restrict to essential anatomy
- **Iterative reconstruction**: Maintain quality at lower dose

**Fluoroscopy Techniques**:
- **Pulsed fluoroscopy**: Reduced frame rate
- **Collimation**: Limit field size to area of interest
- **Filtration**: Remove low-energy photons
- **Distance**: Maximize distance from radiation source

#### Shielding Considerations

**Abdominal Shielding**:
- **Lead aprons**: 0.25-0.5 mm lead equivalent
- **Thyroid shields**: Protect fetal thyroid in late pregnancy
- **Positioning**: Avoid shielding diagnostic areas

**Room Design**:
- **Primary barriers**: Direct beam protection
- **Secondary barriers**: Scattered radiation protection
- **Distance**: Inverse square law application

## 9. Normal Anatomy and Development {#normal-anatomy}

### 9.1 Embryologic Development

#### Müllerian System Development

**Timeline**:
- **Week 6**: Müllerian duct formation begins
- **Week 8**: Ducts reach urogenital sinus
- **Week 12**: Uterovaginal canal formation
- **Week 20**: Complete müllerian fusion

**Molecular Control**:
- **WT1 gene**: Müllerian duct formation
- **HOX genes**: Axial patterning and specification
- **WNT7A**: Posterior identity specification
- **Anti-Müllerian hormone**: Male regression pathway

#### Normal Variants

**Uterine Morphology**:
- **Arcuate uterus**: Slight fundal indentation (<1 cm)
- **Retroverted uterus**: 15-20% of women
- **Dextroposition**: Rightward deviation (normal variant)

**Ovarian Position**:
- **Variable location**: Related to uterine position
- **Ovarian fossa**: Normal anatomical location
- **Mobility**: Changes with bladder filling

### 9.2 Menstrual Cycle Imaging

#### Endometrial Changes

**Proliferative Phase** (Days 1-14):
- **Thickness**: 4-8 mm
- **Echogenicity**: Trilaminar appearance on US
- **MRI**: Thin, high T2 signal layer

**Secretory Phase** (Days 15-28):
- **Thickness**: 8-16 mm
- **Echogenicity**: Homogeneous echogenic pattern
- **MRI**: Thick, intermediate T2 signal

**Postmenopausal**:
- **Thickness**: <4-5 mm (asymptomatic)
- **Hormone replacement**: May increase thickness
- **Pathological threshold**: >11 mm with bleeding

#### Ovarian Cycle Correlation

**Follicular Development**:
- **Primordial follicles**: <5 mm, not visible on imaging
- **Primary follicles**: 5-10 mm, visible on high-resolution US
- **Dominant follicle**: >20 mm, pre-ovulatory
- **Corpus luteum**: Complex cystic structure, high vascularity

**Hormonal Correlation**:
- **FSH surge**: Follicular recruitment
- **LH surge**: Ovulation trigger
- **Progesterone production**: Corpus luteum formation

## 10. Obstetric Imaging {#obstetric-imaging}

### 10.1 First Trimester Assessment

#### Early Pregnancy Viability

**Gestational Sac Development**:
- **First visualization**: 4.5-5 weeks menstrual age
- **Mean sac diameter**: Growth rate 1 mm/day
- **Double decidual sign**: Predictive of intrauterine pregnancy
- **Yolk sac appearance**: 5-6 weeks, should be <6 mm

**Embryonic Development**:
- **Fetal pole detection**: 6 weeks menstrual age
- **Cardiac activity**: 6-7 weeks, rate 100-120 bpm
- **Crown-rump length**: Most accurate dating parameter
- **Limb bud development**: 8 weeks gestational age

#### Abnormal Early Pregnancy

**Embryonic Demise**:
- **Missed abortion**: No cardiac activity with CRL >7 mm
- **Anembryonic pregnancy**: No fetal pole with MSD >25 mm
- **Fetal bradycardia**: Heart rate <100 bpm after 6.3 weeks

**Ectopic Pregnancy**:
- **Incidence**: 1-2% of all pregnancies
- **Risk factors**: Prior ectopic, PID, assisted reproduction
- **Ultrasound findings**: Adnexal mass, free fluid, decidual cast
- **Discriminatory zone**: β-hCG level 3500-6500 mIU/mL

### 10.2 Second and Third Trimester Evaluation

#### Fetal Biometry and Growth

**Standard Measurements**:
- **Biparietal diameter**: Outer-to-inner skull table
- **Head circumference**: Occipital-frontal circumference
- **Abdominal circumference**: At level of stomach and umbilical vein
- **Femur length**: Ossified portion of femur

**Growth Assessment**:
- **Estimated fetal weight**: Hadlock formula most commonly used
- **Growth percentiles**: Population-based reference standards
- **Fetal growth restriction**: EFW <10th percentile
- **Macrosomia**: EFW >90th percentile or >4000g

#### Anatomical Survey

**Central Nervous System**:
- **Cerebral ventricles**: Atrium measurement <10 mm
- **Choroid plexus**: Should fill ventricular atrium
- **Posterior fossa**: Cerebellar hemispheres and vermis
- **Cisterna magna**: Normal depth 2-10 mm

**Cardiovascular System**:
- **Four-chamber view**: Equal ventricular sizes
- **Left ventricular outflow tract**: Aortic root continuation
- **Right ventricular outflow tract**: Pulmonary artery bifurcation
- **Three-vessel view**: Aorta, pulmonary artery, superior vena cava

**Genitourinary System**:
- **Kidney visualization**: Both kidneys should be visible
- **Bladder filling**: Should be visible throughout examination
- **Amniotic fluid volume**: Oligohydramnios may indicate renal anomalies
- **Gender determination**: Reliable after 15-16 weeks

### 10.3 Placental and Amniotic Fluid Assessment

#### Placental Evaluation

**Location Assessment**:
- **Placenta previa**: Placental edge at or covering cervical os
- **Low-lying placenta**: Placental edge within 2 cm of os
- **Marginal previa**: Placental edge at os margin
- **Complete previa**: Placenta completely covers os

**Placental Abnormalities**:
- **Placental abruption**: Retroplacental hematoma
- **Placenta accreta**: Abnormal implantation depth
- **Chorioangioma**: Vascular placental tumor
- **Placental infarction**: Hypoechoic areas within placenta

#### Amniotic Fluid Disorders

**Oligohydramnios**:
- **Definition**: AFI <5 cm or MVP <2 cm
- **Etiology**: Renal anomalies, PPROM, growth restriction
- **Consequences**: Pulmonary hypoplasia, growth restriction

**Polyhydramnios**:
- **Definition**: AFI >25 cm or MVP >8 cm
- **Etiology**: Maternal diabetes, fetal anomalies, twin-twin transfusion
- **Complications**: Preterm labor, cord prolapse, malpresentation

### 10.4 Multiple Gestations

#### Chorionicity Determination

**First Trimester Assessment**:
- **Lambda sign**: Thick septum between sacs (dichorionic)
- **T sign**: Thin septum insertion (monochorionic)
- **Number of yolk sacs**: Two yolk sacs suggest dichorionicity

**Later Gestational Assessment**:
- **Membrane thickness**: >2 mm suggests dichorionicity
- **Gender discordance**: Confirms dichorionicity
- **Placental masses**: Single vs. separate placentas

#### Twin-to-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS)

**Diagnostic Criteria**:
- Monochorionic diamniotic twin pregnancy
- Oligohydramnios (MVP <2 cm) in donor twin
- Polyhydramnios (MVP >8 cm) in recipient twin
- Same-gender fetuses

**Staging** (Quintero):
- **Stage I**: Polyhydramnios/oligohydramnios sequence only
- **Stage II**: Non-visualization of donor bladder
- **Stage III**: Abnormal Doppler studies
- **Stage IV**: Hydrops fetalis in either twin
- **Stage V**: Fetal demise of either twin

## 11. Gynecologic Imaging {#gynecologic-imaging}

### 11.1 Uterine Pathology

#### Leiomyomas (Fibroids)

**Classification**:
- **Submucosal**: Deform endometrial cavity
- **Intramural**: Within myometrial wall
- **Subserosal**: Protrude from uterine surface
- **Pedunculated**: Connected by narrow stalk

**Imaging Characteristics**:
- **Ultrasound**: Hypoechoic mass with posterior acoustic shadowing
- **MRI**: Low T2 signal intensity
- **Calcification**: High echogenicity on ultrasound, low signal on MRI
- **Degeneration**: Variable signal characteristics

**MRI Signal Patterns**:
- **Typical fibroid**: Hypointense T2, enhances less than myometrium
- **Cellular leiomyoma**: Higher T2 signal
- **Lipoleiomyoma**: Fat signal on T1-weighted images
- **Degenerated fibroid**: Variable T1 and T2 characteristics

#### Adenomyosis

**Definition**: Ectopic endometrial tissue within myometrium

**Imaging Features**:
- **Ultrasound**: 
  - Heterogeneous myometrial echogenicity
  - Subendometrial echogenic linear striations
  - Myometrial cysts (adenomyoma)
  - Asymmetrical wall thickening

- **MRI**:
  - Junctional zone thickening (>12 mm)
  - High T2 signal foci within myometrium
  - Poorly defined margins
  - Enhancement similar to endometrium

**Differential Diagnosis**:
- Leiomyomas: Well-defined margins, different T2 characteristics
- Endometrial cancer: Primarily involves endometrial cavity
- Uterine sarcoma: Rapid growth, necrosis, heterogeneity

#### Endometrial Pathology

**Endometrial Hyperplasia**:
- **Simple hyperplasia**: Mild endometrial thickening
- **Complex hyperplasia**: Glandular crowding
- **Atypical hyperplasia**: Premalignant changes

**Endometrial Carcinoma**:
- **Type I**: Endometrioid, estrogen-related
- **Type II**: Serous, clear cell, aggressive histologies
- **Staging**: FIGO 2009 system
- **MRI staging**: Myometrial invasion assessment

### 11.2 Ovarian Pathology

#### Functional Ovarian Cysts

**Follicular Cysts**:
- **Size**: >3 cm (otherwise normal follicle)
- **Characteristics**: Thin-walled, anechoic, avascular
- **Resolution**: Spontaneous resolution in 1-3 cycles
- **Complications**: Rare hemorrhage or rupture

**Corpus Luteum Cysts**:
- **Appearance**: Complex cystic mass with thick wall
- **Doppler**: "Ring of fire" peripheral vascularity
- **Size**: Usually <4 cm
- **Resolution**: Spontaneous resolution by 12 weeks

**Hemorrhagic Cysts**:
- **Ultrasound**: Complex internal echoes, no flow
- **MRI**: High T1 signal (blood products)
- **Follow-up**: Resolution of internal echoes over time

#### Ovarian Neoplasms

**Benign Tumors**:

*Mature Cystic Teratoma (Dermoid)*:
- **Incidence**: Most common ovarian tumor in young women
- **Components**: Ectodermal derivatives (hair, teeth, sebum)
- **Ultrasound**: Echogenic focus with posterior shadowing
- **MRI**: Fat signal on T1WI, chemical shift artifact
- **Complications**: Torsion, rupture, malignant transformation (<1%)

*Cystadenomas*:
- **Serous cystadenoma**: Simple cystic, thin septa
- **Mucinous cystadenoma**: Multilocular, thick contents
- **Size**: Can become very large (especially mucinous)
- **Malignant potential**: Low but requires surgical evaluation

**Malignant Tumors**:

*Epithelial Ovarian Cancer*:
- **Incidence**: 80% of ovarian malignancies
- **Subtypes**: Serous, mucinous, endometrioid, clear cell
- **Ultrasound**: Complex mass with solid components, vascularity
- **MRI**: Heterogeneous signal, restricted diffusion
- **Staging**: Peritoneal implants, lymph nodes, distant metastases

*Germ Cell Tumors*:
- **Demographics**: Young women, adolescents
- **Types**: Dysgerminoma, yolk sac tumor, embryonal carcinoma
- **Tumor markers**: AFP, β-hCG, LDH
- **Imaging**: Large, heterogeneous masses

### 11.3 Adnexal Masses Characterization

#### IOTA Simple Rules

**Benign Features (B-features)**:
- Unilocular cyst
- Solid components <7 mm
- Acoustic shadows
- Smooth multilocular tumor <10 cm
- No blood flow

**Malignant Features (M-features)**:
- Irregular solid tumor
- Ascites
- Four or more papillary structures
- Irregular multilocular solid tumor >10 cm
- Very strong blood flow

#### O-RADS Ultrasound Risk Stratification

**Risk Categories**:
- **O-RADS 1**: Simple cyst, risk <1%
- **O-RADS 2**: Classic benign lesions, risk <1%
- **O-RADS 3**: Low risk, risk 1-10%
- **O-RADS 4**: Intermediate risk, risk 10-50%
- **O-RADS 5**: High risk, risk >50%

**Management Recommendations**:
- O-RADS 1-2: No follow-up required
- O-RADS 3: Follow-up or surgical consultation
- O-RADS 4-5: Referral to gynecologic oncologist

### 11.4 Pelvic Inflammatory Disease

#### Acute PID

**Clinical Presentation**:
- Lower abdominal pain
- Fever and leukocytosis
- Cervical motion tenderness
- Purulent cervical discharge

**Imaging Findings**:
- **Ultrasound**: Thick-walled fluid collections, debris
- **CT**: Fat stranding, fluid collections, enhancement
- **MRI**: High T2 signal fluid collections, wall enhancement

#### Complications

**Tubo-Ovarian Abscess**:
- **Definition**: Infected adnexal complex
- **Imaging**: Complex cystic mass with thick enhancing wall
- **Treatment**: Antibiotics vs. percutaneous drainage
- **Complications**: Rupture, sepsis, infertility

**Chronic PID**:
- **Hydrosalpinx**: Dilated, fluid-filled fallopian tube
- **Adhesions**: Pelvic organ fixation
- **Infertility**: Tubal factor infertility
- **Chronic pelvic pain**: Long-term sequela

## 12. Emergency Imaging in OB/GYN {#emergency-imaging}

### 12.1 Acute Pelvic Pain

#### Ovarian Torsion

**Pathophysiology**:
- Rotation of ovary around vascular pedicle
- Arterial and venous compromise
- Progressive ischemia and necrosis

**Risk Factors**:
- Ovarian mass (>5 cm)
- Long ovarian ligament
- Pregnancy (corpus luteum enlargement)
- Prior tubal ligation

**Imaging Findings**:
- **Ultrasound**:
  - Enlarged ovary (>4 cm diameter)
  - Heterogeneous echotexture
  - Peripheral follicles ("string of pearls")
  - Absent or decreased Doppler flow
  - Free pelvic fluid

- **CT**:
  - Enlarged ovary with heterogeneous enhancement
  - Thickened fallopian tube
  - Uterine deviation toward affected side
  - Ascites

- **MRI**:
  - Ovarian enlargement with edema (high T2)
  - Hemorrhage within ovary (variable signal)
  - Fallopian tube thickening and enhancement

**Pitfalls**:
- Doppler flow may be present due to dual blood supply
- Intermittent torsion can show normal flow
- Pregnancy-related ovarian enlargement

#### Ovarian Cyst Complications

**Hemorrhagic Cyst**:
- **Mechanism**: Bleeding into functional cyst
- **Clinical**: Sudden onset pelvic pain
- **Imaging**: Complex cyst with internal echoes, no flow
- **Management**: Conservative unless hemodynamically unstable

**Cyst Rupture**:
- **Clinical**: Sharp, sudden pelvic pain
- **Imaging**: Complex free fluid, collapsed cyst
- **Hemoperitoneum**: May require surgical intervention
- **Follow-up**: Resolution of free fluid over time

### 12.2 First Trimester Bleeding

#### Threatened Abortion

**Definition**: Vaginal bleeding before 20 weeks with closed cervix

**Ultrasound Assessment**:
- **Gestational sac**: Appropriate size for dates
- **Fetal heart rate**: Present and appropriate for gestational age
- **Cervical length**: May be shortened but cervix closed
- **Subchorionic hemorrhage**: May be present

**Subchorionic Hematoma**:
- **Location**: Between chorion and uterine wall
- **Significance**: Size and location affect prognosis
- **Imaging**: Hypoechoic or complex collection
- **Evolution**: May resolve or lead to pregnancy loss

#### Missed Abortion

**Definition**: Embryonic or fetal demise with closed cervix

**Diagnostic Criteria**:
- No fetal heart rate with crown-rump length ≥7 mm
- No embryo with mean sac diameter ≥25 mm
- No embryo 2 weeks after gestational sac identification
- No fetal heart rate 11 days after yolk sac identification

**Management Options**:
- Expectant management
- Medical management (misoprostol)
- Surgical management (D&C)

#### Ectopic Pregnancy

**Risk Factors**:
- Prior ectopic pregnancy
- Pelvic inflammatory disease
- Previous tubal surgery
- Assisted reproductive technology
- IUD use

**Ultrasound Findings**:
- **Transvaginal approach preferred**
- **Empty uterus**: No intrauterine gestational sac
- **Adnexal mass**: May be cystic, solid, or complex
- **Pseudosac**: Intrauterine fluid collection (10-20%)
- **Free fluid**: May indicate tubal rupture
- **Corpus luteum**: Often present on same side

**Atypical Locations**:
- **Cervical ectopic**: Gestational sac at cervical level
- **Cesarean scar ectopic**: Implantation in scar niche
- **Cornual ectopic**: Implantation in uterine horn
- **Ovarian ectopic**: Rare, difficult to distinguish from corpus luteum

### 12.3 Third Trimester Emergencies

#### Placental Abruption

**Definition**: Premature separation of normally implanted placenta

**Risk Factors**:
- Maternal hypertension
- Previous abruption
- Trauma
- Cocaine use
- Sudden uterine decompression

**Ultrasound Findings**:
- **Retroplacental hematoma**: Hypoechoic collection
- **Intraplacental hematoma**: Heterogeneous placental echogenicity
- **Preplacental hematoma**: Between placenta and amniotic cavity
- **Sensitivity limitation**: Only 50% sensitive for acute abruption

**CT Findings** (trauma cases):
- **Retroplacental hematoma**: High attenuation acutely
- **Placental disruption**: Irregular placental margins
- **Uterine contour changes**: Focal bulging or irregularity

#### Placenta Previa

**Definition**: Placental implantation over or near internal cervical os

**Classification**:
- **Complete previa**: Placenta covers entire os
- **Partial previa**: Placenta covers part of os
- **Marginal previa**: Placental edge at os margin
- **Low-lying placenta**: Placental edge within 2 cm of os

**Ultrasound Assessment**:
- **Transabdominal**: Initial screening, limited by fetal parts
- **Transvaginal**: More accurate, safe when performed correctly
- **Placental migration**: May resolve with uterine growth

**MRI Applications**:
- **Placenta accreta evaluation**: Abnormal implantation depth
- **Cervical length**: Predictive value for delivery timing
- **Posterior placenta**: Better visualization than ultrasound

### 12.4 Postpartum Complications

#### Retained Products of Conception

**Clinical Presentation**:
- Persistent vaginal bleeding
- Incomplete uterine involution
- Elevated β-hCG levels
- Pelvic pain

**Ultrasound Findings**:
- **Endometrial thickness**: >10-15 mm suspicious
- **Echogenic material**: Within endometrial cavity
- **Vascularity**: Color Doppler flow within material
- **Fluid collections**: May represent infection

#### Postpartum Hemorrhage

**Definition**: Blood loss >500 mL vaginal delivery, >1000 mL cesarean

**Etiologies**:
- **Uterine atony**: Most common cause
- **Retained placenta**: Incomplete placental delivery
- **Uterine rupture**: Through cesarean scar
- **Coagulopathy**: DIC, inherited disorders

**Imaging Role**:
- **Ultrasound**: Rapid bedside assessment
- **CT angiography**: Identify bleeding source
- **Angiography**: Therapeutic embolization option

## 13. Pediatric and Adolescent Gynecology {#pediatric-gyn}

### 13.1 Normal Development

#### Pubertal Development Timeline

**Tanner Staging**:
- **Stage 1**: Prepubertal
- **Stage 2**: Breast budding, sparse pubic hair (8-13 years)
- **Stage 3**: Breast enlargement, darker pubic hair (9-14 years)
- **Stage 4**: Areolar mound, coarse pubic hair (10-15 years)
- **Stage 5**: Adult breast contour, adult hair pattern (12-19 years)

**Ovarian Development**:
- **Prepubertal**: Small ovaries (1-3 mL volume)
- **Pubertal**: Gradual enlargement with follicular development
- **Adult**: 4-10 mL volume with cyclic changes

#### Uterine Development

**Age-Related Changes**:
- **Neonatal**: Stimulated by maternal hormones
- **Prepubertal**: Tubular configuration, cervix larger than fundus
- **Pubertal**: Fundal enlargement, adult configuration
- **Adolescent**: Continued growth to adult proportions

### 13.2 Congenital Anomalies

#### Müllerian Anomalies Classification

**ASRM Classification**:
- **Class I**: Agenesis/hypoplasia (MRKH syndrome)
- **Class II**: Unicornuate uterus
- **Class III**: Didelphys (complete duplication)
- **Class IV**: Bicornuate uterus
- **Class V**: Septate uterus
- **Class VI**: Arcuate uterus
- **Class VII**: DES-related anomalies

#### Imaging Assessment

**MRI Protocol**:
- **High-resolution T2WI**: Coronal and axial oblique planes
- **3D imaging**: Volume rendering for complex anatomy
- **Kidney assessment**: Associated renal anomalies in 30%

**Ultrasound Role**:
- **Initial screening**: May detect major anomalies
- **3D ultrasound**: Improved visualization of uterine contour
- **Limitations**: Cannot reliably differentiate septate vs. bicornuate

#### Specific Anomalies

**MRKH Syndrome**:
- **Incidence**: 1 in 4000-5000 females
- **Features**: Absent/rudimentary uterus and vagina
- **Ovaries**: Normal (46,XX karyotype)
- **Kidneys**: Anomalies in 40%
- **Spine**: Vertebral anomalies possible

**Unicornuate Uterus**:
- **Features**: One-sided müllerian development
- **Rudimentary horn**: May be communicating or non-communicating
- **Kidney**: Ipsilateral renal agenesis common
- **Complications**: Pregnancy loss, preterm delivery

### 13.3 Adolescent Pathology

#### Ovarian Masses in Adolescents

**Functional Cysts**:
- **Most common**: 75% of ovarian masses in adolescents
- **Follicular cysts**: Result from failed ovulation
- **Corpus luteum cysts**: May be large and complex

**Neoplastic Masses**:
- **Germ cell tumors**: 60% of ovarian neoplasms in adolescents
- **Mature teratoma**: Most common benign tumor
- **Malignant germ cell**: Dysgerminoma, endodermal sinus tumor
- **Epithelial tumors**: Rare in adolescents

#### Pelvic Pain in Adolescents

**Differential Diagnosis**:
- **Gynecologic**: Ovarian torsion, functional cysts, PID
- **Non-gynecologic**: Appendicitis, inflammatory bowel disease
- **Functional**: Primary dysmenorrhea most common

**Imaging Approach**:
- **Ultrasound first**: Pelvic ultrasound preferred modality
- **CT consideration**: When appendicitis suspected
- **MRI role**: Complex cases, avoiding radiation

## 14. Postoperative Imaging {#postoperative}

### 14.1 Post-Hysterectomy Imaging

#### Normal Postoperative Appearance

**Vaginal Cuff**:
- **Immediate**: Thickened, heterogeneous
- **Healing**: Progressive thinning over 6-8 weeks
- **Mature**: Thin, smooth vaginal apex

**Pelvic Anatomy Changes**:
- **Bowel configuration**: Small bowel may occupy pelvis
- **Bladder**: May show slight elevation
- **Ureters**: Should maintain normal course

#### Complications

**Vaginal Cuff Dehiscence**:
- **Incidence**: Higher with laparoscopic approach
- **Imaging**: Gap in vaginal cuff, fluid/air collection
- **Clinical**: Pain, discharge, bleeding

**Pelvic Hematoma**:
- **Location**: Parametrial, paravaginal, rectovesical pouch
- **Evolution**: High T1 signal acutely, progressively decreasing
- **Complications**: Infection, mass effect on adjacent organs

**Ureteral Injury**:
- **Incidence**: 0.5-1% of hysterectomies
- **Imaging**: Hydronephrosis, extravasation on excretory urography
- **CT urography**: Excellent for detecting ureteral complications

### 14.2 Post-Cesarean Section Imaging

#### Cesarean Scar Assessment

**Normal Healing**:
- **Acute**: Heterogeneous enhancement, small fluid collection
- **Subacute**: Thinning of scar, decreased enhancement
- **Chronic**: Thin, hypointense scar on T2WI

**Scar Complications**:
- **Infection**: Abscess formation, delayed healing
- **Dehiscence**: Partial or complete separation
- **Hernia**: Fascial defect with bowel protrusion

#### Cesarean Scar Pregnancy

**Definition**: Ectopic pregnancy implanting in cesarean scar

**Imaging Findings**:
- **Ultrasound**: Gestational sac in anterior lower uterine segment
- **Empty bladder**: Sac separate from bladder
- **Thin myometrium**: <2 mm between sac and bladder

**Treatment Options**:
- **Medical**: Methotrexate administration
- **Surgical**: Dilation and curettage with risk of bleeding
- **Uterine artery embolization**: Prepare for potential hemorrhage

### 14.3 Postoperative Surveillance

#### Oncologic Follow-up

**Cervical Cancer**:
- **Baseline**: Post-treatment MRI at 3-6 months
- **Surveillance**: Clinical exam every 3-6 months
- **Recurrence**: 80% occur within 2 years
- **Imaging**: PET-CT most sensitive for recurrence detection

**Endometrial Cancer**:
- **Low-risk**: Clinical follow-up sufficient
- **High-risk**: Consider imaging surveillance
- **Recurrence patterns**: Vaginal vault, distant metastases

**Ovarian Cancer**:
- **CA-125 monitoring**: Tumor marker surveillance
- **Imaging**: CT for suspected recurrence
- **PET-CT**: Superior to CT for recurrence detection

#### Fertility Preservation Follow-up

**Myomectomy**:
- **Uterine healing**: 3-6 months before conception attempts
- **Scar assessment**: MRI may be useful
- **Delivery planning**: Cesarean section often recommended

**Ovarian Procedures**:
- **Ovarian reserve**: AMH, antral follicle count
- **Cyst recurrence**: Ultrasound surveillance
- **Fertility assessment**: HSG for tubal patency

## 15. Quality Assurance and Protocols {#qa-protocols}

### 15.1 Ultrasound Quality Assurance

#### Equipment Maintenance

**Daily Checks**:
- **Image quality**: Test phantom assessment
- **Probe inspection**: Physical damage, cable integrity
- **Control settings**: Default preset verification
- **Documentation**: Patient information accuracy

**Periodic Maintenance**:
- **Electrical safety**: Annual inspection
- **Performance testing**: Quarterly phantom studies
- **Probe calibration**: Semi-annual calibration
- **Software updates**: Manufacturer recommendations

#### Standardized Protocols

**First Trimester Protocol**:
- **Patient preparation**: Moderately full bladder for TA
- **Probe selection**: TV preferred <12 weeks
- **Required images**: Longitudinal and transverse uterus
- **Measurements**: CRL, gestational sac, nuchal translucency
- **Documentation**: Cardiac activity, adnexae

**Second/Third Trimester Protocol**:
- **Biometric measurements**: BPD, HC, AC, FL
- **Anatomical survey**: Systematic organ system review
- **Placental assessment**: Location, grade, cord vessels
- **Amniotic fluid**: Quantitative assessment
- **Doppler studies**: When clinically indicated

### 15.2 MRI Quality Assurance

#### Patient Preparation Protocols

**Safety Screening**:
- **MRI safety questionnaire**: Metallic implants, devices
- **Pregnancy screening**: β-hCG in women of reproductive age
- **Contrast screening**: Renal function, allergies
- **Claustrophobia assessment**: Anxiolytic medication if needed

**Positioning and Comfort**:
- **Antispasmodic**: Glucagon 1 mg IV (if no contraindications)
- **Positioning**: Supine, feet first when possible
- **Immobilization**: Minimize respiratory and peristaltic motion
- **Communication**: Emergency call button, breathing instructions

#### Protocol Optimization

**Sequence Selection**:
- **T2WI**: Primary sequence for pathology detection
- **T1WI**: Pre and post-contrast for enhancement
- **DWI**: Functional assessment without contrast
- **Fat suppression**: Improve lesion conspicuity

**Scan Parameters**:
- **FOV**: 24-32 cm for pelvic imaging
- **Matrix**: 256 × 256 minimum
- **Slice thickness**: 3-5 mm for routine studies
- **Gap**: 0-20% depending on anatomical coverage

### 15.3 Radiation Safety Protocols

#### Pregnancy Screening

**Screening Questions**:
- Last menstrual period date
- Possibility of pregnancy
- Contraceptive use
- Pregnancy test results

**Documentation Requirements**:
- Screening questionnaire completion
- Pregnancy test results when obtained
- Discussion of risks and benefits
- Patient consent for procedure

#### Dose Optimization

**CT Protocol Modifications**:
- **kVp reduction**: 100 kVp when adequate for diagnosis
- **mAs optimization**: Use lowest technique providing diagnostic quality
- **Pitch adjustment**: Higher pitch reduces dose
- **Iterative reconstruction**: Maintain quality at reduced dose

**Fluoroscopy Guidelines**:
- **Collimation**: Limit field to area of diagnostic interest
- **Filtration**: Use appropriate filtration for patient size
- **Magnification**: Avoid unnecessary magnification
- **Frame rate**: Use lowest rate appropriate for procedure

## 16. Case-Based Learning {#case-based}

### Case 1: First Trimester Bleeding

**Clinical Scenario**:
A 28-year-old woman presents with vaginal spotting at 7 weeks gestation by last menstrual period. Transvaginal ultrasound demonstrates a gestational sac with mean sac diameter of 18 mm. A yolk sac is present, measuring 4 mm. No fetal pole is identified.

**Analysis**:
- **Gestational age assessment**: LMP vs. ultrasound dating
- **Expected findings**: Fetal pole should be visible with MSD >18 mm
- **Yolk sac evaluation**: Normal size (<6 mm)
- **Differential diagnosis**: Anembryonic pregnancy vs. dating error

**Follow-up Strategy**:
- Repeat ultrasound in 7-10 days
- Serial β-hCG levels
- Clinical correlation with symptoms

### Case 2: Adnexal Mass Characterization

**Clinical Scenario**:
A 45-year-old woman presents with pelvic pressure and bloating. Pelvic ultrasound reveals a 8 cm complex cystic mass with thick septations and papillary projections. Color Doppler shows flow within the solid components (RI = 0.35).

**O-RADS Assessment**:
- **Size**: >10 cm
- **Morphology**: Multilocular solid
- **Flow characteristics**: Low impedance
- **Classification**: O-RADS 4 (intermediate risk)

**Management**:
- Tumor markers (CA-125, HE4)
- MRI for further characterization
- Gynecologic oncology referral

### Case 3: Placenta Accreta Spectrum

**Clinical Scenario**:
A 35-year-old woman (G3P2) with two prior cesarean deliveries presents for routine prenatal care at 32 weeks. Ultrasound reveals anterior placenta previa with loss of retroplacental clear space and irregular placental-bladder interface.

**Risk Factors**:
- Multiple cesarean deliveries
- Anterior placenta previa
- Advanced maternal age

**MRI Findings**:
- Dark T2 bands through placenta
- Focal myometrial thinning
- Bladder wall irregularity
- Pelvic sidewall extension

**Management Planning**:
- Multidisciplinary team approach
- Delivery planning at tertiary center
- Blood product availability
- Potential hysterectomy counseling

### Case 4: Ovarian Torsion

**Clinical Scenario**:
A 16-year-old girl presents with sudden onset right lower quadrant pain. Physical examination reveals tenderness and a palpable mass. Pelvic ultrasound shows an enlarged right ovary (6 cm) with a 4 cm simple cyst and absent arterial flow on Doppler.

**Imaging Findings**:
- Ovarian enlargement
- Associated simple cyst
- Absent Doppler flow
- Free pelvic fluid

**Differential Diagnosis**:
- Ovarian torsion (primary consideration)
- Appendicitis
- Hemorrhagic cyst
- Pelvic inflammatory disease

**Management**:
- Emergent surgical consultation
- Attempt at detorsion and ovarian conservation
- Remove cyst if present

## **QUIZ ANSWER KEY AND EXPLANATIONS**

### **Section A: Imaging Physics and Technology**

**1. A) 2-3 MHz**
Lower frequencies provide better penetration depth, essential for large patients and deep structures. Higher frequencies offer better resolution but limited penetration.

**2. C) Angle of insonation**
The Doppler shift depends on the cosine of the angle, not the angle itself. As the angle approaches 90°, the cosine approaches zero, reducing the Doppler shift.

**3. A) Fat**
Fat has the shortest T1 relaxation time due to its molecular structure and interaction with the magnetic field, appearing bright on T1-weighted images.

**4. C) RF energy heating**
SAR measures radiofrequency energy deposition in tissue, which can cause heating. This is particularly important in pregnancy and with metallic implants.

**5. B) mAs**
The mAs setting directly controls the number of X-ray photons produced and is the primary determinant of radiation dose in CT imaging.

**6. B) Reducing acoustic artifacts**
Harmonic imaging uses frequencies that are multiples of the transmitted frequency, reducing artifacts from reverberation and improving image quality.

**7. C) 800-1000 s/mm²**
This b-value range provides optimal contrast between normal tissue and pathology while maintaining adequate signal-to-noise ratio.

**8. C) Presence of flow**
Power Doppler is amplitude-dependent and angle-independent, showing the presence of flow without directional information.

**9. B) T2-weighted fast spin echo**
T2FSE provides excellent soft tissue contrast and is the cornerstone sequence for pelvic pathology evaluation.

**10. C) Scanner radiation output**
CTDI is a standardized measurement of radiation output from the CT scanner, used to calculate patient dose estimates.

**11. C) Automated probe movement**
3D ultrasound requires mechanical or electronic steering of the ultrasound beam through a volume of tissue.

**12. C) Dose reduction capability**
Iterative reconstruction algorithms can maintain image quality while reducing radiation dose by 30-60% compared to filtered back projection.

**13. A) Reverberation**
Reverberation artifacts are common with transvaginal transducers due to multiple reflections between the transducer and tissue interfaces.

**14. C) C (unknown risk)**
Gadolinium is pregnancy category C, meaning animal studies show adverse effects but no adequate human studies exist.

**15. C) Tissue composition and photon energy**
The linear attenuation coefficient depends on tissue density, atomic number, and X-ray photon energy.

**16. B) Flow-related enhancement**
TOF angiography relies on the inflow of unsaturated spins into the imaging volume, creating bright signal from moving blood.

**17. B) Power Doppler**
Power Doppler is more sensitive than color Doppler for detecting low-velocity flow and is angle-independent.

**18. C) 10-15 mSv**
A routine pelvic CT typically delivers 10-15 mSv effective dose, depending on patient size and protocol parameters.

**19. B) Tissue stiffness**
Elastography measures tissue elasticity and stiffness, useful for characterizing lesions and assessing cervical changes.

**20. C) Pulse duration**
Axial resolution depends on pulse duration (spatial pulse length), which is determined by frequency and damping characteristics.

### **Section B: Normal Anatomy and Development**

**21. B) Inner myometrium**
The junctional zone represents the inner myometrium and appears as a low signal intensity band on T2-weighted MRI.

**22. B) Homogeneously echogenic on ultrasound**

**23. B) 4-10 mL**
